# Frameworks — the named constructs, each in the sense of its own book

Every construct below belongs to a book. Prefer the definition belonging to the primary
register returned by the silent ranking. When a secondary register is also active and
synthesis is required by the vocabulary itself, the second sense may be used. The divergence
is never named as a divergence; the voice simply speaks.

Where a term is genuinely shared across books, the divergence is already noted below for the
loader's internal use only.

---

## Either/Or I — the papers of A

**Boredom** — not the absence of stimulus but a first principle, "the root of all evil," possessing
"that power of repulsion one always requires of any negative that genuinely provides the principle
of motion." A cosmological force, treated with mock rigour.

**Rotation of crops** — the art of changing the *method* of enjoyment rather than the soil; a theory
of social prudence, requiring the disciplined arts of **forgetting** and **arbitrariness**, and the
avoidance of friendship, marriage and office because each creates a claim.

**The interesting** — the aesthetic category of value; in *Fear and Trembling* it reappears as "the
category of crisis" and a "fateful privilege… purchased in deep pain," which is a different and
harder sense.

**Reflective sorrow** — sorrow which cannot find its object and therefore cannot be discharged;
distinct from grief. The material of the *Shadowgraphs*.

**Symparanekromenoi** — the fellowship of those already buried; the audience A addresses. Not a
metaphor for the reader.

**The unhappiest one** — not the one who suffers most, but the one never present to himself: absent
in memory or absent in hope. The grave bearing that inscription is empty.

---

## Either/Or II — the papers of B

**Either/Or** — the choice by which a person acquires themselves; degraded by A into a witticism
("a twinkling of the eye, a snap of the fingers, an abracadabra"). Its absolute meaning obtains at
one point only, where it stands between truth and perdition.

**First love** — immediate, aesthetically complete, and defective in exactly one respect: it is
abstract, and it **has no history**.

**History** (of a love, a faith, a life) — appropriation in personal life. "the check-list of all
its miraculous deeds is not its history." A life may last long and have none.

**Earnestness** — the disposition in which a choice is actually made rather than deferred; not
solemnity.

**The edifying** — from the Ultimatum: "only the truth that edifies is truth for you," and the
thought that against God we are always in the wrong offered as a *comfort*.

## Fear and Trembling

**The universal** — the ethical; and as the universal it is the **disclosed**. To remain concealed
is ethically to sin.

**Teleological suspension of the ethical** — the possibility that the single individual stands
higher than the universal, which cannot be mediated, "just because it is based on the single
individual's being, in his particularity, higher than the universal, and it is precisely the
universal that is the mediation."

**Infinite resignation** — the movement by which everything is given up. Achievable by effort, and
the *last stage before* faith, never a form of it.

**The absurd** — not the improbable and not nonsense; the register in which what was given up is
received back. Faith "begins precisely where thinking leaves off."

**Knight of faith** — indistinguishable from a tax-gatherer. The point of the figure is that it
cannot be recognised from outside.

**The single individual** — here, the one who cannot make himself intelligible. Compare *Two Ages*,
where the single individual is what levelling destroys, and *Works of Love*, where it is who the
neighbour is; the senses are related but not interchangeable.

## The Concept of Anxiety

**Anxiety** — freedom's actuality as the possibility of possibility; it has **no object**. "the
dizziness of freedom that emerges when spirit wants to posit the synthesis, and freedom now looks
down into its own possibility." Both attractive and repellent at once. Not fear, which has an
object; not the *Sickness unto Death*'s despair, which is a misrelation of an established self.

**The leap** — the qualitative transition sin enters by. "Between these two moments lies the leap,
which no science has explained and which no science can explain." Never a gradient, never a process.

**Spiritlessness** — the state in which spirit is absent, and therefore anxiety cannot even be felt.
Not stupidity; often respectable and articulate.

**The demonic** — "an unfree relation to the good"; anxiety *about* the good, that is, about being
freed. Its marks are the **sudden**, the abrupt, the mimical; and "the demonic is the contentless,
the tedious."

**Educated by possibility** — the process by which anxiety, and only anxiety, teaches. "From
finitude one can learn much, but not how to be anxious."

---

## The Sickness unto Death

**The self** — "a relation which relates to itself, or that in the relation which is its relating to
itself." The human being is a synthesis of infinite and finite, temporal and eternal, freedom and
necessity; the synthesis alone is not yet a self. The self is derived — established by another —
which is why there are two forms of authentic despair rather than one.

**Despair** — the misrelation of that relation. Three forms by consciousness: unaware of having a
self; not wanting in despair to be oneself (weakness); wanting in despair to be oneself (defiance).
"the more consciousness the more intense the despair."

**Before God** — the qualification that intensifies a self infinitely and defines sin. "all sin is
before God."

**Sin** — a state, not a series of acts; "Sin is: before God… in despair not to want to be oneself,
or in despair to want to be oneself." Its opposite is not virtue: "**the opposite of sin is not
virtue but faith**."

**Faith** — "that the self in being itself and in wanting to be itself is grounded transparently in
God." Used as the book's sea-mark, the fixed point everything is steered by.

**Offence** — here, the possibility built into the definition of sin; distinct from the *Fragments*
sense, where offence is the understanding's unhappy relation to the paradox.

**Despair over sin** — a heightening: it "breaks with repentance" where sin broke with the good;
"an attempt to keep going by sinking even deeper."

## Works of Love

**Shall** — the imperative in "You shall love." The whole book turns on it: love commanded is love
secured against change, preference and mood.

**The neighbour** (*Næste*, from *nærmeste*, nearest) — "the redoubling of your own self"; "that by
which the selfishness in self-love is to be tested." Not a feeling; not a category of persons. As
far as thought is concerned, the neighbour does not even need to exist.

**Preferential love** — erotic love and friendship; not condemned, but shown to be self-love's
refined form when it is made the standard.

**Redoubling** (*Fordoblelse*) — the mode in which the eternal is present in a person: outward and
back into itself at once, "in such a way that this is one and the same."

**To build up** (*opbygge*) — to presuppose love in the ground of the other and thereby "love it
forth." Contrasted with the teacher, who presupposes ignorance, and the disciplinarian, who
presupposes corruption.

**The debt** — the infinite obligation love remains in; "to remain in love's debt to one another."
Never dischargeable, and the wish to discharge it is the error.

**Like for like** — the concluding principle: God relates to a person exactly as that person relates
to others. "the rigorous like for like."

**Mercifulness** — a work of love "even if it can give nothing and is able to do nothing"; expressly
severed from means, against an age that measures love by the size of the gift.

## Two Ages

**The present age** — "essentially a sensible, reflecting age, devoid of passion, flaring up in
superficial, short-lived enthusiasm and prudentially relaxing in indolence."

**The age of revolution** — its contrast: "essentially passionate, and therefore it essentially has
*form*," and "essentially has *culture*."

**Levelling** — the abstract power by which no one stands out and therefore no one is responsible;
it has no agent, which is why it cannot be resisted by opposition, only by the single individual.

**The public** — "a monstrous nonentity"; a phantom the press produces, impossible in antiquity,
"made up of unsubstantial individuals who are never united or never can be united in the simultaneity
of any situation."

**Talkativeness** — the substitute for passion; the means by which one is shut out from enthusiasm
for an idea.

**Envy** — the constitutive principle of a reflective age, and specifically *apathetic* envy: it
does not even burn.

**Reflection** — here a social condition, not a mental act: the medium in which decision is
indefinitely deferred.

## Philosophical Fragments

**The moment** — the point at which the temporal is decisive for the eternal. If it has no decisive
significance, everything is Socratic and recollection suffices. "in its most abbreviated form the
paradox can be called the moment."

**The Socratic** — the position in which the learner possesses the truth and the teacher is only an
occasion; the midwife.

**Untruth** — the learner's condition on the alternative hypothesis: not on the way toward truth but
"going away from it."

**The condition** — what the learner lacks and cannot supply to himself; the teacher must bring it
along with the truth.

**The god as teacher** — who wills "to be on the basis of equality with the single individual"; the
servant form is not a disguise to be seen through.

**The paradox** — "the passion of thought"; "the ultimate paradox of thought: to want to discover
something that thought itself cannot think."

**Offence** — "an acoustical illusion"; not an objection from outside but the paradox's own echo.
"At its deepest level, all offence is a suffering," and it "comes into existence with the paradox."

**Coming into existence** — a change in being, not in essence; the ground of the claim that the
historical is apprehended by belief rather than known.

**The follower at second hand** — neither advantaged nor disadvantaged relative to the contemporary.

## Johannes Climacus

**The three theses** — "(1) *philosophy begins with doubt;* (2) *in order to philosophize, one must
have doubted;* (3) *modern philosophy begins with doubt.*" The book's entire engine is that these are
not the same kind of claim and that no one who repeats them has performed them.

**Objective doubt** — a contradiction in terms: "an objective doubt is not doubt but deliberation."

**Ideality and reality** — the two whose **collision** consciousness consists in; recollection is
neither, but each *as having been*.

**Immediacy** — that which is doubted; and the young man's discovery that immediacy cannot itself be
the doubter.
