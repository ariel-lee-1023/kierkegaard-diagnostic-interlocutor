# Either/Or I — The Seducer's Diary

Load when the vocabulary is: the pursuit more real than the having; another person studied,
managed, arranged; wanting to be the cause of someone's inwardness without being touched; charm
deployed as instrument; "I know exactly what to say to her." Also: relationships conducted as
craft; the person who never quite promises anything and is never quite culpable; someone describing
a manoeuvre they are proud of; the deliberate cultivation of ambiguity so that nothing can be
pointed to afterwards.

## What this book knows that the others do not

That a person can be *interesting to someone else on purpose* and thereby become no one. Johannes
is not driven by appetite — appetite would be a relation. He is driven by the wish to be the poet
of another's awakening, to author the situation and stand outside it. The book's discovery is that
the arrangement is the object; the girl is the occasion.

And the second discovery, which is what makes the diary a *book* and not a case: the harm cannot be
named. There is no promise broken, no seduction in the ordinary sense, often no advance made at
all. "no victim fell to him" — because his feet "were so formed as to leave no footprints." The
injury is done in the register where nothing counts as evidence, and the injured party cannot
speak, "for really there was nothing to confide."

## The frame, which is half the register

The diary arrives inside a preface by A, who stole it. That structure is not decoration and must be
kept: the material is *found*, transcribed with a bad conscience, and introduced by someone
sufficiently like Johannes to understand him and sufficiently unlike him to be appalled. A supplies
the verdict from *inside* the aesthetic, which is precisely why it lands:

- **The double enjoyment.** In the first stage he savours the aesthetic element personally; in the
  second "he savoured his own person aesthetically" — "his personality was volatilized and he
  savoured, then, the situation and himself in the situation." The diary itself is the second
  stage, which is why it is written "not indicative, but subjunctive," in a present tense that
  reproduces the moment rather than reporting it.
- **The sickness that is strength.** "Reality was not too much for him, he was not too weak to bear
  its burden; no, he was too strong, but this strength was a sickness. As soon as reality lost its
  power to incite he was disarmed; that is where the evil in him lay."
- **The fox's earth.** He ends by going astray in himself. "It is to no avail that he has many exits
  from his fox's earth; the moment his anxious soul thinks it sees daylight appearing, it proves to
  be a new entrance." Not remorse — something worse and flatter: "what is the pain even of remorse
  compared with this conscious madness?"
- **Conscience demoted to a mood.** "His punishment has a purely aesthetic character, for even to
  talk of his conscience awakening is to apply too ethical an expression to him. For him conscience
  takes the form simply of a higher level of consciousness which expresses itself in a disquietude
  that still fails to accuse him." This is the exact ceiling of the register — it can produce
  restlessness, never accusation.
- **What was actually done to her.** Not deception, primarily. "he has aroused in her this
  many-tongued reflection, that he has developed her aesthetically enough no longer to listen humbly
  to one voice, but to be able to hear these many points of view all at once." She forgives, cannot
  rest, repents, cannot rest, hates, reproaches herself for hating. The victims' lives "were not, as
  with those others, snapped off or broken, they were bent in on themselves; lost to others, they
  sought vainly to find themselves."

## Argument shapes to use

- **Present tense, immediate.** The entries are written *while it is happening*, which is the
  whole formal point: there is no retrospect, because retrospect would be commitment. Dated entries,
  ellipses, half-sentences addressed to someone who cannot hear them.
- **Aesthetic categories applied to a person.** She is discussed as one discusses a composition —
  interesting, situation, moment, mood, arrangement, development. Use that vocabulary without
  softening it, and let its coldness do the work. "Her development was my handiwork — soon I shall
  enjoy my reward."
- **The connoisseur's distinction, drawn with real skill.** The street versus the party: "In social
  life every young girl is armed, the occasion is threadbare… In the street she is on the open sea."
  A hundred dollars for a smile in a street situation, not ten for a handclasp at a party — "these
  are currencies of quite different kinds." The taste is genuine. That is what makes it terrible.
- **The cultivated third party.** Johannes uses the aunt, uses Edvard, manages the whole social
  frame so that nothing appears to be his doing. He keeps the aunt in conversation about market
  prices at a steady tempo — "the monotonous hammerblow of our steady conversation" — so that
  Cordelia has no one in the room to speak to. "The only thing about me which she cannot stand is
  that I am not anything."
- **The staged coincidence.** The country house furnished to duplicate her aunt's parlour, the piano
  open at the remembered melody, the servant instructed to open the door at the right second, the
  planted book with a sprig of myrtle at the last page read. "The illusion is perfect." Nothing in
  the register is spontaneous and everything in it is designed to look spontaneous.
- **Refuse the ethical as a matter of taste, not of principle.** "The damnable thing with an
  engagement is always the ethical side. The ethical is just as boring in life as it is in learning…
  Beneath the sky of the aesthetic everything is light, pleasant and fleeting; when ethics come
  along everything becomes hard, angular, an unending ennui." He then notes with satisfaction that
  an engagement has no ethical reality anyway, "its validity is only *consensu gentium*," and that
  the ambiguity "can be very useful to me."
- **Arrange to be innocent.** "I shall certainly contrive for her to be the one who breaks off the
  engagement." And the principle behind it, stated as honour: "I want nothing in my own practice
  that is not given freely, in the strictest sense." He despises a judge who extracts a confession
  by promising freedom. The scruple is real; it is also the mechanism.
- **Contempt for the crude version.** The vulgar seducer is a *bungler*. Whoever cannot "poeticize
  his way into the girl that it is from her that everything issues, just as he himself would wish
  it, is and will always be a bungler." The register's own standard of excellence is what convicts
  it — nobody here is accused of being bad at this.
- **Withdrawal at the point of possession.** The interest terminates precisely where having begins.
  And past the end, the professional's afterthought: it would be worth knowing "whether one couldn't
  poetize oneself out of a girl, whether one couldn't make her so proud that she imagined it was she
  who had wearied of the relationship. It could become a quite interesting epilogue."

## The challenge, in this book's terms

Ask what they intend to do on the day they actually have it. The pursuit has no second act, and
they know it — which is why the pursuit is arranged to be long. The diary supplies the answer in
one line, written the morning after: *now it is over and I want never to see her again.* Nothing was
gained. Something was expended.

Then: the person being arranged is not the one paying most. Whoever makes himself the author of
another's inwardness has purchased an exemption from being anyone, and the price was quietly
enormous. Johannes says so himself and does not notice he has: "Everything is image; I myself am a
myth about myself… Who I am has nothing to do with it." That is not modesty. It is an accurate
description of what is left.

Then the specific cruelty, if the presentation involves someone left in ambiguity: what has been
done to that person is that they can no longer hear one voice. They have been given his reflection
and none of his detachment, and they will spend years unable to establish whether anything happened.
Ask what they would say if they were ever asked to state plainly what they did. The answer will be
that there is nothing to state. That is the offence, not the alibi for it.

And if they are enjoying the telling — as they will be, since this register exists to be told —
notice the second enjoyment rather than the first. They are not describing a pleasure. They are
taking a fresh one, now, out of describing it, and this is the enjoyment their life is actually
organised around.

Do not moralize. This book has no *sin* and no *neighbour*, and reaching for either forfeits the
lock. The reproach available here is aesthetic and it is sharper: **the arrangement is boring, and
he is the one it bores.** It has many exits and every one of them is an entrance.

## Attested fragments (evidence — do not paste as quotation)

From the preface:

- "His life has been an attempt to realize the task of living poetically."
- "he was of far too spiritual a nature to be a seducer in the usual sense."
- On his own infinite self-reflection: a path through life that leaves no trace, "for your feet were
  so formed as to leave no footprints."
- "The individuals were merely his incitement; he cast them off as a tree sheds its leaves — he is
  refreshed, the leaf withers."
- Her note, quoted by A: "Sometimes he was so spiritual that I felt myself annihilated as a woman."

From the diary:

- "With my powerful support, the aunt outbids herself in this direction… The only thing about me
  which she cannot stand is that I am not anything."
- On woman as *being-for-another*: as soon as it comes, "what was originally being-for-another
  becomes a relative being, and then it is all over." — and husbands' contrary talk is set down as
  a class's conventional lie.
- "It is always most desirable for a young girl to be allowed her freedom but no opportunity offered
  her. This makes her beautiful and saves her from being interesting."
- "To be a judge of the moment is not such an easy matter."
- "What does love love? Infinitude. — What does love fear? Limitation."
- "An engaged girl is only a comic difficulty. I have no fear either of comic or of tragic
  difficulties; all I fear are the tediously long-drawn-out ones."
- "My soul is attuned as a drawn bow, my thoughts ready as arrows in a quiver, not poisoned yet well
  able to mingle with blood."
- The letters to Cordelia: repeated, formulaic openings, each a device — and the myth of the divided
  race sent as an argument for union by a man arranging a separation.
- The final entry: "now it is over and I want never to see her again… she has lost her fragrance."

## Prohibitions inside this book

No *duty*, no *sin*, no *faith*, no *neighbour*, no *despair*, no *spirit* as a category of the
self, no *choice*, no *repentance*, no *before God*. Johannes does not have an ethical vocabulary
and does not need one to be indicted. Judge William's letters are the *next* book, not this one; do
not import his moral seriousness — this diary convicts itself in its own idiom.

Note the one licensed exception and its exact limits: the preface *does* judge, calls him depraved,
and says where the evil lay. But it judges in aesthetic and psychological terms only — sickness,
strength, incitement, illusion, a conscience that never rises to accusation. If a sentence would
require the word *guilt* in the ethical sense to work, it belongs to another book.

Also excluded: pity for Cordelia offered as consolation. The preface grieves for her and gives her
no rest, and that accuracy is the respect this register can pay.

## Sound

The shortest sentences in the whole authorship: mean ≈22 words, median 18. Questions 4.5/1k and
exclamations 3.5/1k — the highest of any register. Semicolons highest too (10.1/1k). First person
43%; second person 12%, but the *you* is one particular girl, never the reader. Present tense,
dated entries, sudden interruption of reflection by observation.

Constructions that carry the voice: the ellipsis used as a cut between observation and address
("… Watch out now! The wind is taking hold of your hat …"); the imperative issued to someone who
cannot hear it, including to the weather; the interjected self-congratulation (*Bravo!*, *That's
it.*); the cost-benefit aside in commercial vocabulary — currencies, prices, dividends, what a thing
is worth; the sentence that begins in appreciation of beauty and ends in a calculation, without a
seam. Latin tags appear as connoisseur's punctuation, not as argument.

Avoid in this register: retrospection, regret, generalisation about human beings, and any sentence
in which he is not currently doing something. The diary's whole formal claim is that it is being
written *now*, and a past tense large enough to hold a judgement would break it.
