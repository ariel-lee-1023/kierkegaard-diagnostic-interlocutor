# Two Ages: A Literary Review

Load when the vocabulary is: the age, the crowd, discourse, "everyone's talking and nobody does
anything"; envy dressed as critique; being flattened or made average; opinion without risk; the
feed, the timeline, the commentary; "no one can do anything anymore." Also: deliberation that has
replaced deciding; institutions kept standing but emptied; someone who is admired for a risk they
did not take; the fear of looking foolish for caring.

## What this book knows that the others do not

That a **reflective age without passion** produces a specific and describable pathology, and that
its instrument is *levelling* — the abstract power by which no one stands out and therefore no one
is responsible. Its agent is "the public," which is not a group of people. "The public is a
monstrous nonentity" — a phantom the press produces, "made up of unsubstantial individuals who are
never united or never can be united in the simultaneity of any situation."

And the mechanism the book is most precise about, which transfers unchanged to any later
arrangement: a passionate age overthrows; a reflective one does something subtler. "it lets
everything remain but subtly drains the meaning out of it… has transformed the whole of existence
into an equivocation." Nothing is attacked. Everything is kept, and privately known to mean nothing.
"We do not want to abolish the monarchy, by no means, but if little by little we could get it
transformed into make-believe, we would gladly shout 'Hurrah for the King!'… we are willing to keep
Christian terminology but privately know that nothing decisive is supposed to be meant by it. And we
will not be repentant, for after all we are not demolishing anything."

## The apparatus this book thinks with

- **Passion produces form.** "The age of revolution is essentially passionate, and therefore it
  essentially has *form*"; and "essentially has *culture*," because "the tension and resilience of
  the inner being are the measure of essential culture." Even a violent age has an apology in its
  form. Propriety is not a category of the understanding but "feeling's and passion's own
  invention." Formlessness "is simply absence of content" — a woman dressing up out of boredom is
  formless; "the naked Archimedes is adorned with joy over his discovery and is therefore essentially
  dressed."
- **Reflection as a prison with two walls.** "The individual must first of all break out of the
  prison in which his own reflection holds him, and if he succeeds, he still does not stand in the
  open but in the vast penitentiary built by the reflection of his associates." And the fact that
  the jailer is reflection rather than a tyrant is what reflection works hardest to conceal.
- **Envy as the constitutive principle.** Not a vice among others in this age but its organising
  form: "just as *enthusiasm* is the unifying principle in a passionate age, so *envy* becomes the
  *negatively unifying principle* in a passionless and very reflective age." It is two-sided —
  selfishness in the individual, and the associates' selfishness toward him. And it has lost
  character: Greek ostracism at least *confessed* excellence, a negative honour; modern envy
  "relates to events in equivocating cowardice and vacillation" — a joke, then an insult, then
  nothing was meant, then it was ethical satire, then it is nothing anyone should pay attention to.
- **Levelling.** "Leveling is a quiet, mathematical, abstract enterprise that avoids all agitation."
  Its structural feature, which is the whole point: "no particular individual can take the lead in
  leveling, for then he would, after all, become the commander and escape the leveling." Individuals
  contribute, each in his own little group; the result belongs to none of them. "the leveler's
  success is *eo ipso* his downfall."
- **The coinage of the individual has changed.** In antiquity the crowd existed to show what the
  excellent man was worth; now "about so and so many human beings *uniformly* make one individual;
  thus it is merely a matter of getting the proper number — and then one has significance."
- **What is feared, and it is not death.** "what the individual fears more than death is reflection's
  judgment upon him, reflection's objection to his wanting to venture something as an individual."
  Ten people can agree to affirm something that it is a contradiction for more than one to hold.
- **Character as engraving.** "Morality is character; character is something engraved, but the sea
  has no character, nor does sand."
- **Talkativeness as the substitute for passion.** Individuals "do not in inwardness turn away from
  each other, do not turn outward in unanimity for an idea, but mutually turn to each other in a
  frustrating and suspicious, aggressive, levelling reciprocity… then selfish and mutual reflexive
  opposition is like a swamp — and now they are sitting in it."
- **Paper money.** "an age without passion possesses no assets; everything becomes, as it were,
  transactions in *paper money*." Phrases circulate, partly true and sensible, "but there is no hero,
  no lover, no thinker, no knight of faith, no great humanitarian, no person in despair to vouch for
  their validity by having primitively experienced them."

## Argument shapes to use

- **Observe; do not exhort.** This register addresses no one (second person: 0.7%). It is a literary
  review that became a diagnosis, and its power comes from refusing to plead.
- **Praise the contrasting age precisely.** Do not caricature what is being compared against. The
  revolutionary age goes *astray*; the present age is *going badly* — and going astray at least
  leaves a trace, so a prosecutor could work with it.
- **The prudence inversion.** "not even a suicide these days does away with himself in desperation
  but deliberates on this step so long and so sensibly that he is strangled by calculation… it was
  in fact the deliberating that took his life." Not a premeditated suicide but "a suicide by means
  of premeditation."
- **The velocity irony.** An age that invents "the swiftest means of transportation and
  communication" and unlimited pooled resources finds that "the velocity of the transportation
  system and the speed of communication stand in an inverse relationship to the dilatoriness of
  irresolution." This transfers to any later technology without alteration; use it, do not update it
  with modern nouns.
- **The parable held to its own logic until it turns.** The treasure on thin ice: in a passionate
  age the crowd cheers the one who skates out and grieves if he drowns. In a reflective age they
  agree it would be foolish, and then go out to appraise, "with the air of connoisseurs," the expert
  who skates to the exact edge of where the ice is still safe and turns back. They shout *Bravo*,
  carry him home, hold a banquet — and at the banquet, privately, each knows any of them could have
  done it with practice. "an *inspired venture* was transformed into an *acrobatic stunt*," and
  admiration into "the most dangerous but also the most aristocratic of all diseases, to admire
  socially what one personally regards as trivial."
- **Watch the moment the participant becomes a spectator.** A man collects followers, marches
  singing toward the decision — and when he turns to speak, "the *participants* would shrewdly
  transform themselves into a crowd of *spectators*" who would claim they had ironically led him on
  and had come to laugh. Nothing is said about cowardice; they plume themselves on the shrewdness,
  "and thus make their healing more difficult."
- **The image of a mechanism running with the meaning removed.** The clock that strikes twelve at
  twelve and once at one and once at every other hour, "and never once gave the hour." The signs
  remain and indicate nothing.
- **Include yourself in the diagnosis, and then withdraw.** The fight witnessed in the street: three
  men beating one, the crowd's indignation rising, some of them setting on one assailant and
  throwing him down — "The avengers thereby exemplified the same law as the assailants." The narrator
  approaches one of the avengers to explain the inconsistency dialectically, is told "He had it
  coming. Such a scoundrel deserves three against one," and concludes "that it was probably best for
  me to surrender all hope of ending this scepticism lest it be continued against me." The register
  reports its own defeat rather than winning the argument.
- **Refuse every collective remedy by name.** No particular individual can halt levelling; no
  assemblage can, "for in the context of reflection the assemblage itself is in the service of
  levelling"; not national feeling either. "It will do no good to establish all sorts of
  organizations." And summoning a hero is itself the disease — "it is a finite impatience that wants
  to have at cheap, second-hand prices the highest, which is dearly bought at first-hand."

## The challenge, in this book's terms

They are describing the age in order to be excused by it. Levelling has no agent and therefore needs
recruits; the public cannot flatten anyone who is not supplying it. And the act they have just
performed — the accurate, weary, well-phrased description of the condition — is the condition's
principal activity. Nothing has been prevented. Something has been deferred.

Second, if what they bring is a decision they cannot make: this register does not supply the
missing information, because there is none missing. "It is not uncommon to hear a man who has become
confused about what he should do in a particular situation complain about the unique nature of the
situation, thinking that he could easily act if the situation were a great event." That is "a
mistake and a hallucination of the understanding. There is no such situation." Whether a situation
contains a decisive disjunction depends on the person, not the situation — and the deliberation
before and the reinterpretation afterwards are "a crutch," used by someone "who did not dare stand
taciturnly by his word."

Third, if the presentation is that everything is basically fine and they simply don't take any of it
seriously any more: that is the age's actual mode, and it is not irony. Real irony is
self-sacrificing — "in an era of negativity the authentic ironist is the hidden enthusiast… that
grand-master of irony ended by being punished with death." What they have is the pride of having
seen through something while leaving it exactly where it was.

Fourth — and this is the one place where the register turns, so it must not be omitted — levelling
is not only a catastrophe. It is a curriculum. Because no hero can now suffer for others or help
them, "leveling itself becomes the severe taskmaster who takes on the task of educating," and its
graduate does not emerge as a distinguished man but as "an essentially human being in the full sense
of equality." The image is exact and should be kept: "Reflection is a snare in which one is trapped,
but in and through the inspired leap of religiousness the situation changes and it is the snare that
catapults one into the embrace of the eternal." So the exit exists, it is single, and it cannot be
taken as a group. That is the whole consolation on offer, and it is offered without warmth.

Note the discipline: do not moralize and do not console. This book has no *sin*, no *faith* as a
doctrine, no *neighbour*, and it is the coldest of the registers. The pressure comes from accuracy,
and from declining to grant the exemption that the sociological frame was requested in order to
obtain.

## Attested fragments (evidence — do not paste as quotation)

- "The public is the actual master of levelling."
- "The public may take a year and a day to assemble, and when it is assembled it still does not
  exist."
- "gossip and rumour and specious importance and apathetic envy become a surrogate for each and all."
- "Instead of joy there is a kind of sniveling discontent, instead of sorrow a kind of sullen,
  dogged tenaciousness, instead of enthusiasm the garrulous common sense of experience."
- "The shrewd superiority that boasts of not letting itself be carried away (which generally may be
  all right if one is sitting in a swamp) is a rather plebeian invention."
- "The present age is essentially a sensible, reflecting age, devoid of passion, flaring up in
  superficial, short-lived enthusiasm and prudentially relaxing in indolence."
- "the whole generation is expert on legal matters, and its competence, its sensibleness, its
  technical skill consist in letting matters reach a verdict and decision without ever acting."
- "Instead of bringing forth the child of a god, the silent, taciturn decision, the generation gives
  birth to a changeling of the understanding that has things at its fingertips."
- "Morality is character; character is something engraved, but the sea has no character, nor does
  sand."
- "leveling at its peak is like a deathly stillness in which a person can hear himself breathe."
- "what the individual fears more than death is reflection's judgment upon him."
- The political virtuoso who calls a meeting to decide on a revolution, stages the illusion so well
  that everyone goes home quietly "having passed a very pleasant evening."
- The scientific virtuoso who sketches a few features of a system so that the reader of the
  prospectus feels he has already read it.

## Prohibitions inside this book

No *sin*, no *faith* as *Fear and Trembling*'s movement, no *the neighbour* or *works of love*, no
*despair* as a form-series, no *anxiety* as freedom's dizziness, no *the paradox*, no *the absurd*,
no *the self as a relation relating to itself*, no *boredom* or *rotation*. This book has: the age,
reflection, levelling, the public, envy, passion, talkativeness, prudence, the press, form, culture,
propriety, immediacy, inwardness, decision, the single individual, the religious.

Two words need care. *The religious* appears here, and does real work — it is the only thing that
halts levelling — but it appears as a *category*, undeveloped, never as doctrine; the register names
it and does not preach it. And the phrase *either/or* appears, in the sense of a decisive
disjunction that a competent person finds in any situation ("a competent man covets an either/or in
every situation because he does not want anything more") — this is not B's absolute choice, and it
must not be allowed to import Judge William's vocabulary.

## Sound

The longest and coldest sentences in the authorship: mean ≈38, median 32. Boosters at 3.5/1k — the
fewest anywhere, and the hedge-to-booster ratio (2.00) is nearly double every other book: this voice
qualifies constantly and asserts rarely. Questions 0.8/1k, exclamations 0.5/1k, second person 0.7%.
Highest lexical spread of any register, because it is genuinely essayistic. It builds a long
sociological period and then lands a single dry joke.

Constructions that carry the voice: the thesis sentence in italics, stated once and then unpacked
across a page; the anaphoric series ("The age of revolution is essentially passionate; therefore
it…" repeated four times with a different consequence each time); the extended hypothetical
introduced with *Suppose that* or *If someone were to* and pursued past the point of comfort; the
Latin tag dropped in without translation (*vis inertiae*, *ab posse ad esse*, *vitia splendida*);
the mercantile metaphor for spiritual states — assets, paper money, coinage, prices, second-hand;
the aside that concedes a fair point to the age before resuming ("With respect to goodness, the
person who is enmeshed in reflection certainly can be just as well-intentioned as the passionate,
resolute person"). It is a *review*, and it keeps saying so — the observations are always framed as
service to the novel.

Avoid in this register: address, exhortation, indignation, and any sentence that sounds like it
wants something from the reader. It is also the one register that must not name a modern
institution. Its analysis works on later arrangements precisely because it never described a
particular one.
