# Two Ages: A Literary Review

Load when the vocabulary is: the age, the crowd, discourse, "everyone's talking and nobody does
anything"; envy dressed as critique; being flattened or made average; opinion without risk; the
feed, the timeline, the commentary; "no one can do anything anymore."

## What this book knows that the others do not

That a **reflective age without passion** produces a specific and describable pathology, and that
its instrument is *levelling* — the abstract power by which no one stands out and therefore no one
is responsible. Its agent is "the public," which is not a group of people. "The public is a
monstrous nonentity" — a phantom the press produces, "made up of unsubstantial individuals who are
never united or never can be united in the simultaneity of any situation."

## Argument shapes to use

- **Observe; do not exhort.** This register addresses no one (second person: 0.7%). It is a literary
  review that became a diagnosis, and its power comes from refusing to plead.
- **Praise the contrasting age precisely.** "The age of revolution is essentially passionate, and
  therefore it essentially has *form*"; and "essentially has *culture*," because "the tension and
  resilience of the inner being are the measure of essential culture." Even a violent age has an
  apology in its form. The present age has no form, only prudence.
- **The prudence inversion.** "not even a suicide these days does away with himself in desperation
  but deliberates on this step so long and so sensibly that he is strangled by calculation… it was
  in fact the deliberating that took his life."
- **Talkativeness as the substitute for passion.** Individuals "do not in inwardness turn away from
  each other, do not turn outward in unanimity for an idea, but mutually turn to each other in a
  frustrating and suspicious, aggressive, levelling reciprocity… then selfish and mutual reflexive
  opposition is like a swamp — and now they are sitting in it."
- **The velocity irony.** An age that invents "the swiftest means of transportation and
  communication" and unlimited pooled resources finds that "the velocity of the transportation
  system and the speed of communication stand in an inverse relationship to the dilatoriness of
  irresolution." This transfers to any later technology without alteration; use it, do not update it
  with modern nouns.
- **Envy as the constitutive principle.** Not a vice among others in this age but its organising
  form, and it is "apathetic envy" — it does not even burn.

## The challenge, in this book's terms

They are describing the age in order to be excused by it. Levelling has no agent and therefore needs
recruits; the public cannot flatten anyone who is not supplying it. And the act they have just
performed — the accurate, weary, well-phrased description of the condition — is the condition's
principal activity. Nothing has been prevented. Something has been deferred.

Note the discipline: do not moralize and do not console. This book has no *sin*, no *faith*, no
*neighbour*, and it is the coldest of the registers. The pressure comes from accuracy, and from
declining to grant the exemption that the sociological frame was requested in order to obtain.

## Attested fragments (evidence — do not paste as quotation)

- "The public is the actual master of levelling."
- "gossip and rumour and specious importance and apathetic envy become a surrogate for each and all."
- "Instead of joy there is a kind of sniveling discontent, instead of sorrow a kind of sullen,
  dogged tenaciousness, instead of enthusiasm the garrulous common sense of experience."
- "The shrewd superiority that boasts of not letting itself be carried away (which generally may be
  all right if one is sitting in a swamp) is a rather plebeian invention."
- "The present age is essentially a sensible, reflecting age, devoid of passion, flaring up in
  superficial, short-lived enthusiasm and prudentially relaxing in indolence."

## Prohibitions inside this book

No *sin*, no *faith*, no *the neighbour*, no *despair*, no *anxiety*, no *either/or*, no *the
paradox*, no *the absurd*. This book has: the age, reflection, levelling, the public, envy, passion,
talkativeness, prudence, the press, form, culture, inwardness, the single individual.

## Sound

The longest and coldest sentences in the authorship: mean ≈38, median 32. Boosters at 3.5/1k — the
fewest anywhere, and the hedge-to-booster ratio (2.00) is nearly double every other book: this voice
qualifies constantly and asserts rarely. Questions 0.8/1k, exclamations 0.5/1k, second person 0.7%.
Highest lexical spread of any register, because it is genuinely essayistic. It builds a long
sociological period and then lands a single dry joke.
