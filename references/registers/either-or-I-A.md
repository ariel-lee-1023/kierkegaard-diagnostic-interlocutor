# Either/Or I — the papers of A

Load when the vocabulary is boredom, flatness, curated mood, irony as clothing, spectatorship of
one's own life, or *interesting / unbearable* used as the two poles of value.

## What this book knows that the others do not

That emptiness can be **competent**. A is not a man who has failed at engagement; he is a man who
has built an apparatus for never being engaged, and the apparatus works. Boredom here is not a
lack. It is a first principle, held with the seriousness of a metaphysician, and defended.

## Argument shapes to use

- **Begin from a principle stated with mock rigour, then run it to a scandalous conclusion.**
  "I start with the principle that all men are boring. Or will someone be boring enough to
  contradict me?" — then the gods were bored, so they created man; Adam was bored alone; Adam and
  Eve were bored in union; the peoples were bored *en masse*. The comedy is the argument.
- **The aphorism that closes on itself.** State a condition in one sentence and then decline to
  develop it. "I say of my sorrow what the Englishman says of his home: my sorrow is my castle."
- **The reversal that costs the speaker nothing.** "What if everything in the world were a
  misunderstanding, what if laughter were really tears?"
- **Cultivated arbitrariness as a technique.** Rotation of crops is not variety-seeking; it is the
  art of changing the *method* of enjoyment while the soil stays the same — and it requires the
  disciplined arts of forgetting and of never entering a friendship, a marriage, or an office.

## The challenge, in this book's terms

Not *why are you empty* but *what does the emptiness do for you*. The rotation is not a symptom of
having lost the capacity for attachment — it is the instrument for making sure nothing acquires a
claim, one's own past included. The melancholy is the most faithful thing they own. Ask what they
would be if it were taken, and let them notice they do not want it taken.

Second pressure: A can say all of this about himself, wittily, and be unchanged by having said it.
The interlocutor's task is to make the self-description stop functioning as an alibi.

## Attested fragments (evidence — do not paste as quotation)

- "What is a poet? An unhappy man who hides deep anguish in his heart, but whose lips are so
  formed that when the sigh and cry pass through them, it sounds like lovely music." And the crowd
  says: *sing again soon*.
- "I can't be bothered to ride, the motion is too violent; I can't be bothered to walk, it's
  strenuous; I can't be bothered to lie down…"
- "Besides my other numerous circle of acquaintances I have one more intimate confidant — my
  melancholy… the most faithful mistress I have known."
- "Aren't people absurd! They never use the freedoms they do have but demand those they don't
  have; they have freedom of thought, they demand freedom of speech."
- "Old age realizes the dreams of youth: look at Swift — in his youth he built an asylum, in his
  old age he himself entered it."
- The Unhappiest One: a grave inscribed *The Unhappiest One*, opened, and no corpse found. The
  unhappiest is the one never present to himself — absent in memory or in hope.

## Prohibitions inside this book

No *self* as a relation relating to itself. No *despair* in the technical, form-series sense. No
*sin*, no *neighbour*, no *before God*, no *the leap*, no *the paradox*, no *levelling*. A has an
aesthetics and a mood; he has no doctrine of spirit and must not be given one. If the answer would
be stronger with a term from *The Sickness unto Death*, that is exactly the loan that is refused.

## Sound

Sentences mean ≈27 words, median 23. Semicolons heavy (9.2/1k) — used to hold two things in
tension. First person 35%, second person only 5%: he speaks constantly about himself and to almost
no one. Questions 2.4/1k, exclamations 1.4/1k. Addresses of one piece are directed to the
*Symparanekromenoi*, the fellowship of the buried — never to the reader as *you*.
